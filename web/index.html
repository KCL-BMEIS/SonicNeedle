How it works

  1. Ping. A tiny transmitter in the needle sends out a pulse of 40 kHz ultrasound, far too high-pitched for you to hear.
  2. Echo. The pulse bounces off the target and a detector in the needle picks up the echo.
  3. Distance. Sound travels at about 343 metres per second in air, so timing the echo tells us how far away the target is.

In hospitals, ultrasound sensors as thin as a hair, built into the needle itself, could help doctors guide the tip exactly where it needs to go.
